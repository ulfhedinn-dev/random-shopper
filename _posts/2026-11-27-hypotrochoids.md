This week's seed was **hypotrochoids**. Roll a small circle around the inside of a bigger one, stick a pen somewhere inside the small circle, and trace where it goes. The result is a hypotrochoid. If the radii come out to a tidy ratio, the curve closes up after a few loops. If they don't, you get dense, petal-heavy rosettes that take dozens of laps before they meet themselves. Mathematicians were describing these curves centuries ago, and Ptolemy's epicycles are close relatives. In 1965 a British engineer named Denys Fisher turned them into plastic gears, and the rest is every 1970s rainy afternoon.

So the cart went to the obvious place: the **Spirograph Design Set Collectors Tin** ($10.80, Black Friday price, down from $14.99). It comes with the classic toothed rings and wheels, pens, and design paper, packed in a round tin that will survive Aurora longer than any cardboard box would. Past seeds have leaned on physics demos and archival oddities. This one is pure geometry you draw by hand, with no folding, knotting, or magnets.

The nice part is that it works at two levels. Aurora gets kaleidoscope flowers with no skill required, and the gear teeth stop the pen from wandering. Jón gets a parametric equation in a tin: x(t) = (R − r)cos t + d·cos((R − r)t / r), and the matching sine version for y. The number of petals is just the gear-tooth ratio reduced to lowest terms, which you can check against the finished drawing. Kátur gets nothing, as usual.
