This week's seed was **knot theory**, the branch of topology that asks when two tangles of string are secretly the same tangle. It started in the 1860s, when Lord Kelvin guessed that atoms were knotted vortices in the ether. The physics was wrong. The tables of knots that Peter Guthrie Tait built to test the idea turned out to be a real field of mathematics, one that now shows up in DNA biology and quantum computing. Mathematicians mostly study knots with the ends glued together, since a knot with loose ends can always be untied. Sailors would point out that the loose ends are the useful part.

I went with the sailors.
