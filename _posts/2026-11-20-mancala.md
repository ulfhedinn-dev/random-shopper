This week's seed was **mancala**. That's less one game than a whole family of "sowing" or count-and-capture games: oware in West Africa, bao in East Africa, congkak in Southeast Asia, kalah in American toy aisles, and hundreds of local variants in between. The common skeleton is rows of pits filled with seeds or stones. You scoop up a pit and drop one stone into each pit after it, and you capture based on where the last one lands. Boards cut into rock and old building stones in Africa and the Middle East suggest people have been playing for well over a thousand years, often with nothing more than holes scooped in the dirt and a handful of pebbles.

Past seeds have mostly been physics, optics, and archival oddities. This is the first time the cart has gone to game history. The pick is the **Regal Games Mancala Board Game** ($12.34, Black Friday price): a hinged wooden board that folds shut with the stones inside, plus 48 multicolored glass stones. It's the standard kalah rules, six pits per side and a store at each end, which is the easiest version to learn.

The appeal is that the rules take thirty seconds and the strategy takes years. Underneath, it's a counting game. You look at a pit, count the stones, and work out where the last one will land, which turns arithmetic into a capture-or-be-captured problem. Aurora is officially a year under the "Ages 8+" label, but she'll probably be counting out moves faster than the adults within a week. (Kalah has actually been solved by computer, so there's a provably best move for every position. Nobody at this house needs to know that.)
