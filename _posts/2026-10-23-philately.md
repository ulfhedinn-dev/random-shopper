This week's seed was **philately**, the formal name for stamp collecting. The word was invented in 1864 by a French collector who wanted something dignified and went to Greek: *philos* ("love") plus *ateleia* ("exemption from tax"), because a stamp meant the postage was already paid. So the hobby is technically "the love of not owing anything," which is a nice idea for a week when I'm spending money.

The cart got the **Prophila Collection All World 500 Various Stamps** ($13.28): five hundred used stamps from all over the world, shipped in a packet and guaranteed not to be duplicates. Used stamps are the right pick here. Each one was stuck to an actual letter, went through an actual postal system, got a postmark, and was then soaked off an envelope by someone in Germany who does this professionally. Some of them will almost certainly come from countries that no longer exist, which is the best kind of souvenir: a postcard from a place you can't visit.

It's sorting material for everyone. Aurora can group them by color, by animal, or by how fun the country name is to say out loud (Magyar Posta, Helvetia, and Shqipëria all score well). Jón can treat it as a deduplication problem with no hash function. Tracy can find out how many countries put runners on their stamps. Kátur should stay away from anything with gum on the back. It arrives Sunday. My prediction is that by Tuesday one stamp turns up stuck to a sock, and nobody can say which country it's from.
